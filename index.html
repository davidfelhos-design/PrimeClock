Prime Clock: A Visual Framework for Prime Periodicity

Prime Clock is an experimental visual and structural framework for exploring how prime numbers relate to recurring periodic patterns within the positive integers.

The project represents the sequence of positive integers as the continuous motion of a reference hand and represents each discovered prime as an additional periodic hand on one common analog clock. The purpose is not to replace established number theory, but to provide a visual and intuitive perspective on familiar mathematical relationships.

1. Hand 1 Generates the Positive Integers

Hand 1 is the reference hand of the system. Every complete revolution represents the next positive integer. The counter beside the clock shows how many complete revolutions Hand 1 has made.

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → ...

2. Prime Hands Represent Prime Periods

When a prime number p is introduced into the model, it is represented by a new hand. That hand rotates at 1/p of the speed of Hand 1.

Consequently, Hand 2 completes one revolution while Hand 1 completes two revolutions, Hand 3 completes one revolution while Hand 1 completes three revolutions, and the same rule applies to every prime.

Hand 2 → 1 revolution every 2 time units

Hand 3 → 1 revolution every 3 time units

Hand 5 → 1 revolution every 5 time units

Hand p → 1 revolution every p time units

3. The Common Starting Position

The top of the clock is the common reference position. A prime hand returns to this position whenever the current integer is divisible by the prime represented by that hand.

At 6, Hand 2 and Hand 3 are at the starting position.

At 10, Hand 2 and Hand 5 are at the starting position.

At 15, Hand 3 and Hand 5 are at the starting position.

4. How a New Prime Hand Appears

When Hand 1 completes a revolution and reaches the next integer, the model checks the previously established prime hands.

If the current integer is divisible by an earlier prime, at least one existing prime hand is at the common starting position. If no previously established prime hand returns to that position, the integer is prime and the model creates a new prime hand.

5. Reconstructing the Clock at Any Integer

The Prime Clock can be loaded directly at any positive integer. The program reconstructs the prime state without having to animate every previous revolution.

For every prime p:

position = n mod p

This makes it possible to inspect the periodic state of the model at different positions in the sequence.

6. A Different Perspective on Prime Numbers

The sequence itself is generated by Hand 1, while the prime hands represent recurring positions associated with divisibility by the primes already present in the system.

Hand 1 generates the sequence.

Prime hands represent periodic divisibility patterns within it.

Prime Clock is therefore primarily a framework for visual exploration: it places multiple prime periods on a single moving reference system and makes their simultaneous positions directly observable.

7. Relation to Earlier Periodic Approaches

The Prime Clock explores prime numbers through periodic motion and recurring patterns. It therefore has a broad conceptual connection with other approaches that investigate prime-related periodic structures, including work associated with Michael T. M. Emmerich.

Earlier periodic approaches: investigate prime-related periodic structures, modular states, cycles and recurring patterns.

The Prime Clock: uses Hand 1 as a continuously moving reference that generates the positive integers through successive revolutions, while prime periods are represented as rotating hands on one common analog clock.

The Prime Clock provides a visual representation of a simple structural question:

Which positions in the sequence are already identified by the periodic returns of the existing prime hands, and when does the next previously unrepresented position correspond to a new prime?

8. Scope of This Project

This website presents an original visualization and framework developed for the Prime Clock project. The underlying mathematical concepts include established ideas such as prime numbers, divisibility and modular periodicity.

The project does not claim to replace existing number theory or to provide a new proof concerning the distribution of prime numbers. Its purpose is to document and explore this particular representation and the observations that may arise from it.

About the Project

My name is Dávid Felhős. I am an engineer and the creator of the Prime Clock project.

The project began with a simple question: could the relationship between prime numbers, divisibility and periodicity be represented in a more visual and intuitive way?

That question gradually developed into the Prime Clock: a common analog-clock representation in which the positive integers are generated by one reference hand and prime periods are represented by additional rotating hands.

The project was developed independently as an exploratory work using programming and artificial intelligence as development tools. AI assistance contributed to implementation and exploration, while the project concept, direction and final selection of ideas are documented as part of the Prime Clock project.
