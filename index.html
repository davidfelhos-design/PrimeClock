How the Prime Clock Works

The Prime Clock is a visual model for exploring the appearance of prime numbers through a system of rotating hands. The model starts with a single hand representing the number 1.

1. Hand 1 Creates Positive Integers

Hand 1 is the reference hand of the system. Every complete revolution of Hand 1 represents the next positive integer. The counter beside the clock shows how many complete revolutions Hand 1 has made.

In this sense, the sequence of positive integers is generated simply by the passage of time:

1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 → ...

2. Prime Hands

Whenever a new prime number appears, a new hand is created. A prime hand with value p rotates at 1/p of the speed of Hand 1.

Therefore, the hand representing the prime 2 completes one revolution while Hand 1 completes two revolutions. The hand representing 3 completes one revolution while Hand 1 completes three revolutions. The same rule continues for every newly discovered prime.

Hand 2 → 1 revolution every 2 time units

Hand 3 → 1 revolution every 3 time units

Hand 5 → 1 revolution every 5 time units

Hand p → 1 revolution every p time units

3. The Starting Position

The top of the clock represents the common starting position. A prime hand returns to this position whenever the current integer is divisible by that prime.

At 6, Hand 2 and Hand 3 are at the starting position.

At 10, Hand 2 and Hand 5 are at the starting position.

At 15, Hand 3 and Hand 5 are at the starting position.

4. How a New Prime Appears

When Hand 1 completes a revolution and reaches the next integer, the model checks all previously discovered prime hands.

If at least one existing prime hand is also at the starting position, the number is already covered by the periodic structure of an earlier prime.

If no previous prime hand is at the starting position, a new prime hand is created.

5. Jumping to Any Revolution

The Prime Clock can also be loaded directly at any positive integer.

When a revolution number is entered, the clock reconstructs the complete prime state as if the animation had been running continuously from revolution 1.

All prime hands up to the selected number are created and their positions are calculated directly.

For every prime p:

position = n mod p

This means that the clock does not need to animate every previous revolution in order to reconstruct its state.

6. A Different Perspective on Prime Numbers

The sequence itself is generated by Hand 1. Each revolution of the reference hand creates the next positive integer.

Prime numbers appear when none of the periodic positions created by earlier prime hands reaches that integer.

Hand 1 generates the sequence.

Prime hands mark periodic positions within it.

7. Relation to Emmerich's Work

The Prime Clock shares an important mathematical mechanism with Michael T. M. Emmerich's Prime Clockwork: a new prime period appears when none of the previously established prime periods returns to its reference position.

Emmerich's Prime Clockwork: focuses on autonomous prime clocks and extends the periodic model to modular states, cycle counts and prime-based representations.

The Prime Clock: uses Hand 1 as a continuously moving generator of the positive integers and represents prime periods as rotating hands on one common analog clock.

The Prime Clock therefore asks:

Which positions are already reached by the existing prime periods?

And where does a new prime period need to appear?

Who I Am

My name is Dávid Felhős. I am an engineer who created this project as a hobby, with the help of artificial intelligence.

This project started simply because I became curious about prime numbers.

I wanted to find a visual and intuitive way to understand why prime numbers appear where they do. That curiosity gradually developed into the Prime Clock.

For me, this model helped to demystify one of the most fascinating aspects of prime numbers: their seemingly mysterious appearance within the sequence of positive integers.

The project is not the result of a formal mathematical research programme. It is an experimental visualization created by an engineer exploring an idea with curiosity, programming and the assistance of AI.
